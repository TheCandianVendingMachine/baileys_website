I keep getting rejected by firms: Let's build their product

home github personal blog tech blog

It's not about what you know, it's who you know. That advice rings true, whether your job is blue collar, white collar, or if you decide to enter a life of crime. It is also burning me right now due to my misfortune in entering the software industry.

I've been coding since I was 13, and I am good at what I do. I know that I can pick up concepts quickly. I know that I don't need LLMs to augment my ability (although I do use them to get through boilerplate); but convincing a firm of that against every other candidate is impossible. I have become frustrated in my search.

The path I see my peers take (I'm a late graduate at 25, my peers are 21-23) is that they get offered a full time role at the internship they were lucky to grab. I think this is the most common path, because companies are given grants to take on interns; it's low risk for them. And if they happen to find a competent undergrad (obviously a tall order), they will be willing to take the non-existent risk to promote them to a junior role.

This was the path I was on until the company I was working for was unable to hire me. It's not because I wasn't good, mind you; I had 4 work terms there. It just so happened that my last work term (also my last term of university) started one week after the company had lay-offs. I've been hunting their job postings since, to try and nab a role, but I was never fortunate to find it.

Where does that leave me? Jobless, and unable to break into the industry. My old company loved my work, but economics didn't work out. Any company that is hiring pass over me for reasons I do not know; I have no clue what stage any of my applications are in because of the hellscape that is modern job applications. And I am rapidly losing sanity because of it.

I am a confident woman. I'm trans, so it's tough to exist in industry [although that should be enough qualifications to land me any role], but my skills persist. Being the cocky bitch I am, I think I can easily recreate at least a subset of the main product these firms-who-have-rejected me create, and god dammit I am going to do that.

Join me as I log my adventures in product recreation. I will go into what I learned, the roadblocks I ran into, and how I persisted. I hope that this can be a document which shows how hard the modern job search has become, since if I can succeed, how many others out there can too?
